# Customer-Centric Legal Claim Website

## Lead Analyst Research + Website Content Blueprint

> **Purpose:** This document is a build-ready content and UX blueprint
> for a consumer-facing legal claim / mass-tort intake website. It is
> written for direct use with Windsurf/Copilot during website
> development.
>
> **Positioning assumption:** The website is consumer-facing. It should
> help an injured person understand what may be happening, decide
> whether to request a free review, and understand what happens after
> submission. It should not read like a lead marketplace, a law-firm
> sales page, or a generic legal template.
>
> **Important:** Competitor language has been analyzed for structure,
> messaging, conversion patterns, and trust signals. The proposed copy
> below is original and should not be copied from competitor websites.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Executive Direction

## The strategic shift

The existing benchmark websites are primarily built around one action:

**"Check if you qualify."**

That is useful, but a stronger consumer experience should answer the
questions a real claimant has before they submit:

1.  **Could what happened to me be related to a claim?**
2.  **Am I in the right place?**
3.  **Do I have to pay anything?**
4.  **Who am I actually speaking to?**
5.  **What happens to the information I submit?**
6.  **Will I be pressured to hire someone?**
7.  **What information do I need?**
8.  **How quickly will someone contact me?**
9.  **Does submitting this form mean I already have a case?**
10. **What happens after I am connected with a law firm?**

The website should therefore be built around:

> **Clarity → Trust → Relevance → Simple Review → Human Follow-Up**

Not:

> **Fear → Settlement Promise → Long Form → Submit**

# 2. Competitive Analysis

## 2.1 Skye Claim Connect

### Core positioning

Skye Claim Connect presents itself as a private claim-screening and
legal-intake service. Its homepage uses a direct promise around helping
qualified claimants connect with legal teams through a "fast, secure,
and client-first" experience.

Its primary conversion language includes:

-   Check My Claim Eligibility
-   Free Review
-   Start Your Consultation
-   Secure Claim Review
-   Verify Eligibility In Minutes
-   Free Case Review

It also prominently displays active categories such as Depo-Provera,
Roblox, Rideshare, Talc, Ozempic, MVA, and WTC.

### What Skye does well

**1. Immediate consumer relevance**

The hero immediately tells a visitor what the website is for. The
visitor does not need to understand the company first.

**2. Case-category visibility**

Putting case types near the top helps visitors self-identify quickly.

**3. Multiple conversion points**

The site repeats the review CTA throughout the page instead of making
the user scroll back to the hero.

**4. Trust-oriented form**

The form includes a TrustedForm element and explicit consent language.

**5. FAQ intent**

The FAQ addresses the most common questions around qualification,
compensation, deadlines, cost, and required information.

### What should NOT be copied

The site has several messaging problems that a stronger consumer website
can avoid:

-   Some process headings sound unnatural: "Plan With Success,"
    "Detailed Cost Estimate," and "We Care About The Rest."
-   The statement that the team has a "98% success rate" creates a
    potentially confusing impression for a lead-generation/intake
    service and should not be used without a very strong, verifiable
    basis and legal review.
-   "Receive the settlement you deserve" sounds like a result promise.
-   "Available 24/7 for emergencies" is poorly aligned with a legal
    intake website unless there is genuinely an emergency response
    operation.
-   The site mixes legal intake, legal advice-like language,
    compensation expectations, and intake operations.
-   The homepage form asks for a street address before establishing
    enough trust/value for that level of information.

### Strategic lesson

**Borrow:** category discovery, repeated CTA, simple review concept,
visible privacy/consent, TrustedForm.

**Improve:** plain English, honest expectations, progressive disclosure,
fewer promises, stronger explanation of what happens after submission.

# 3.2 FindTheFirm

## Core positioning

FindTheFirm is more explicitly consumer-oriented around the idea:

> "You deserve justice. We help you find it."

It explains that it is not a law firm and functions as a legal intake /
lead-generation service that connects people with participating law
firms.

### What FindTheFirm does well

**1. Strong emotional entry point**

The homepage begins with the claimant's situation rather than the
company's capabilities.

**2. Explains the business model**

The site clearly says it is not a law firm and explains the connection
between claimant and participating law firms.

**3. Strong mass-tort education**

The "What Is a Mass Tort?" section reduces confusion between a mass tort
and a class action.

**4. Simple three-step process**

The flow is easy to understand:

-   Quick online form
-   Free case review
-   Get connected

**5. Objection handling**

The FAQ directly answers:

-   Is this a law firm?
-   Does it cost anything?
-   Is my information safe?
-   How do I know if I qualify?

**6. Consumer proof**

The testimonials are designed to reduce uncertainty and make the
experience feel human.

### Weaknesses / opportunities

**1. Some claims are too broad**

Statements such as "top-rated law firms," "thousands," or implied
certainty around legal outcomes should only be used when substantiated.

**2. "Life-changing" framing**

This is emotionally strong but can become promotional or
outcome-oriented in a sensitive legal context.

**3. Form trust could be improved**

A consumer should understand exactly who may receive the information
before submitting.

**4. The website is inconsistent across case pages**

Some case links currently resolve to "Coming Soon" pages, while other
pages have much more detailed intake experiences. A production site
should avoid this inconsistency.

**5. The privacy language is evolving**

The homepage now provides more specific disclosure about participating
firms and service providers than older versions of the site. That level
of transparency should be treated as a model for the new site.

### Strategic lesson

**Borrow:** claimant-first tone, education, transparent business model,
FAQs, testimonials, simple process.

**Improve:** consistency, disclosure clarity, evidence-backed trust
claims, and less outcome-oriented language.

# 4. Additional Market Benchmarks

## Direct2Attorney

Direct2Attorney is a useful benchmark for honesty.

Its strongest messaging is not "we will get you compensation."

Instead, it explains:

-   the review takes about two minutes
-   there is no cost to submit
-   the service is not a law firm
-   participating firms decide independently whether to review or accept
    a case
-   submitting information does not guarantee a claim
-   a consumer may be contacted if preliminary criteria are met

### Lesson

A consumer website becomes more credible when it is willing to say:

> "We cannot promise that you have a case. We can help you find out
> whether a participating legal team may be able to review it."

That is stronger than promising a settlement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Specter Legal

Specter Legal uses a strong "tell us what happened" model.

Its process is:

1.  Tell us what happened.
2.  Share key case details.
3.  We review your case.

It also emphasizes:

-   free
-   confidential
-   no obligation
-   review before routing

### Lesson

"Tell us what happened" is psychologically easier than "prove you have a
case."

The new site should make the first action feel conversational.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Legal Help Justice

Legal Help Justice uses a very direct consumer proposition:

-   free case evaluation
-   no fees unless you win
-   confidential and secure
-   all 50 states

### Lesson

Trust points should be visible before the form, not hidden below the
fold.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## InjuryLaw360

InjuryLaw360 uses a useful "not sure?" pathway:

> "I just know I was hurt."

This is a powerful UX pattern because many consumers do not know the
legal name of their claim.

### Lesson

The new website should not force a visitor to diagnose their own legal
category.

Include:

> **Not sure which case applies? Tell us what happened.**

# 5. Recommended Brand Positioning

## Consumer promise

The website should not position itself as "the company that sells legal
leads."

The consumer should experience it as:

> **A clear first step toward understanding whether they may have a
> legal claim.**

## Recommended positioning statement

> **A simpler way to start a legal claim review.**

Supporting message:

> If you were injured, exposed to a dangerous product, or affected by an
> event that may have legal implications, you can request a free review.
> We help collect the basic details and, when appropriate, connect you
> with a legal team that handles cases like yours.

## Brand personality

The website should feel:

-   Calm
-   Human
-   Clear
-   Respectful
-   Private
-   Reassuring
-   Professional
-   Helpful

Avoid:

-   Aggressive
-   Fear-based
-   "Get rich"
-   "Huge settlement"
-   "Guaranteed compensation"
-   Excessive urgency
-   Excessive legal jargon
-   Fake authority

## Section: Why people are seeking legal review

Explain the underlying issue in plain English.

Do not write like a law brief.

Use:

> "\[Product\] has been the subject of legal investigations concerning
> \[plain-English issue\]. If you experienced \[condition\], a legal
> team may want to review your circumstances."

Avoid:

> "\[Product\] caused your condition."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Section: Who may qualify

Use a clear checklist.

### You may want to request a review if:

-   You used or were exposed to \[product/event\]
-   You experienced \[condition\]
-   The exposure occurred during the relevant period
-   You meet the applicable age / location / diagnosis / usage
    requirements
-   You can provide additional information if requested

Use "may qualify" rather than "you qualify."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Section: What information may be useful

Examples:

-   Approximate dates
-   Product or medication name
-   Diagnosis
-   Treatment history
-   Location
-   Employment or exposure history
-   Medical records, if available

Important:

> **You do not need to have every document ready to start a review
> unless the case-specific requirements say otherwise.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Section: What happens after you submit

1.  We receive your information.
2.  The basic details are reviewed against current criteria.
3.  If your situation appears relevant, a participating legal team may
    contact you.
4.  The law firm decides independently whether to review or accept the
    matter.
5.  You decide whether you want to move forward.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Section: Important

> Submitting information does not guarantee that you have a legal claim,
> that a law firm will accept your case, or that you will receive
> compensation.

This is important because it creates trust instead of selling certainty.

# 21. FAQ

## Is this a law firm?

No. This website provides legal intake and/or referral services. It does
not itself provide legal representation or legal advice.

## Does it cost anything to request a review?

There is no charge to submit a request for a free initial review through
the website. If a law firm agrees to represent you, its fee arrangement
will be explained separately.

## Does submitting the form mean I have a case?

No. Submission does not guarantee that you have a legal claim, that a
law firm will accept the matter, or that you will receive compensation.

## Will someone call me?

You may be contacted by the intake service, participating law firms, or
other parties identified in the applicable consent disclosure, depending
on the inquiry and the permissions you provide.

## Is my information private?

Your information is handled according to the site's Privacy Policy and
consent disclosures. Be specific about who may receive information and
why.

## Do I need medical records to start?

That depends on the case. Many reviews can begin with basic information,
while certain matters may require documentation or medical confirmation
before a legal team can evaluate them fully.

## How long does a case review take?

Initial screening times vary by case type and volume. A request for
review does not mean that a law firm has accepted representation.

## What if I am not sure which case I have?

That's okay. Choose "I'm not sure" and describe what happened. The
intake process can help identify the closest category.

## What if the event happened years ago?

Some legal claims have strict deadlines that vary by state and case
type. If you believe you may have a claim, it is better to request a
review promptly.

## Will I have to go to court?

Not necessarily. Whether a case involves litigation, settlement
discussions, a deposition, trial, or another process depends on the
specific matter and the law firm representing you.

## Does a free review guarantee compensation?

No. No website should promise a settlement or specific compensation
amount based only on an intake form.

# 28. UX RULES FOR WINDSURF IMPLEMENTATION

## Rule 1 --- Never trap the visitor

Every page should provide a clear route to:

-   learn
-   review
-   contact

## Rule 2 --- Keep CTA language consistent

Primary CTA:

**Start My Free Review**

Secondary CTA:

**See If I Qualify**

Avoid changing the CTA to a different promise on every section.

## Rule 3 --- Progressive disclosure

Do not put a 20-field form in front of a first-time visitor.

Use a multi-step flow.

## Rule 4 --- Mobile first

Most consumer acquisition traffic will likely be mobile.

Prioritize:

-   large tap targets
-   sticky CTA
-   short sections
-   fast form loading
-   clear progress indicator
-   minimal typing
-   select buttons where appropriate

## Rule 5 --- No fake urgency

Use real deadlines only when verified for the specific case and
jurisdiction.

Avoid generic:

> "Act now before it's too late!"

Prefer:

> "Some legal claims have deadlines that vary by state and case type. If
> you believe you may qualify, consider requesting a review promptly."

-   Where are the privacy and terms pages?
-   Is the consent language readable?
-   Is the case information current?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 33. CONTENT TO AVOID

Do not use unsupported claims such as:

-   "98% success rate"
-   "We guarantee compensation"
-   "You will receive a settlement"
-   "Get millions"
-   "Guaranteed approval"
-   "We will win your case"
-   "You definitely qualify"
-   "Top-rated attorneys" without substantiation
-   "Thousands of people have already won" without evidence
-   "No risk" if there are meaningful consequences or disclosures not
    explained

Avoid manipulative phrases:

-   "Don't let them get away with it"
-   "The company doesn't want you to know"
-   "You could be owed millions"
-   "Claim your money now"
-   "Act before they close the case"

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 34. CONTENT VOICE

## Write like this

> out. Start with what happened, and we'll help you understand the next
> step.

## Not like this

> Our cutting-edge legal intake ecosystem empowers injured consumers to
> unlock maximum compensation through a streamlined qualification

The first sounds human.

The second sounds like marketing software.

# 44. FINAL STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATION

The strongest version of this website should not try to sound like a law
firm.

It should sound like the **calm first step before the law firm**.

Skye Claim Connect demonstrates the value of strong case discovery and
repeated conversion points.

FindTheFirm demonstrates the value of explaining the consumer journey,
the mass-tort concept, the business model, and the "not a law firm"
distinction.

Direct2Attorney demonstrates the value of transparent expectations.

Specter Legal demonstrates the value of "tell us what happened" language
and review-before-routing.

The new site should combine these strengths while removing:

-   settlement hype
-   unsupported success statistics
-   vague "expert team" claims
-   confusing process language
-   unnecessary first-step form fields
-   aggressive urgency
-   ambiguous data-sharing language

## The central message

> **You don't need to know whether you have a case. You just need to
> know what happened.**

That is the consumer proposition the entire site should reinforce.
